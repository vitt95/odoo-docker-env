======================================================================
2. DAY-1 NON-NEGOTIABLE CONSTRAINTS
======================================================================

The Day-1 system MUST be realistically implementable on modest hardware.

Day-1 assumptions:

- one LLM
- Qwen3.5 9B
- one physical machine
- one GPU if available
- modest CPU
- modest RAM
- limited VRAM
- PostgreSQL as primary relational database
- local or containerized services
- no mandatory Kubernetes
- no mandatory service mesh
- no mandatory distributed database
- no mandatory multi-region deployment
- no mandatory multi-GPU infrastructure
- no mandatory cloud LLM
- no mandatory cloud infrastructure
- no mandatory distributed control plane
- no mandatory microservice architecture

The system must be capable of being developed and operated by a small
engineering team.

The architecture must therefore avoid requiring an enterprise-grade
infrastructure platform merely to operate the initial product.

5. THE MOST IMPORTANT INSTRUCTION
## ARCHITECTURE MUST BE THE RESULT OF RESEARCH
======================================================================

Do NOT start by writing the architecture.

Do NOT simply transform this prompt into a system design.

Do NOT assume that the proposed Control Plane / Governance Plane /
Runtime / Resource Plane decomposition is correct.

Do NOT assume that a modular monolith is correct.

Do NOT assume that microservices are wrong.

Do NOT assume PostgreSQL is sufficient for every responsibility.

architecture.

       ↓
    DAY-1 DESIGN
       ↓
    FUTURE EVOLUTION
       ↓
    SELF-CRITIQUE

The architecture must be the RESULT of this process.

7. ARCHITECTURE CHALLENGE PROCESS
======================================================================

After identifying the leading architecture, attempt to DISPROVE it.

Perform a section:

"Attempt to Falsify the Recommended Architecture"

Ask:

- What assumptions could make this architecture wrong?
- At what workload would it stop being appropriate?
- What failure mode would expose its weakness?
- What requirement would force another architecture?
- What future feature would make this design problematic?
- What operational burden could become unacceptable?
- What security issue could invalidate it?
- What scalability bottleneck could appear?
- What migration problem could emerge?

Then determine whether the recommendation survives the challenge.

If it does not survive, revise the recommendation.
