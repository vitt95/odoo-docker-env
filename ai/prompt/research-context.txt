# RESEARCH CONTEXT — ARCHITECTURAL ORIENTATION

## 1. RESEARCH DIRECTORY

Before executing the first architecture prompt, inspect and read the entire:

```text
/research
```

directory and its relevant contents.

The `research` directory contains authoritative research, standards, specifications, technical references and architectural material relevant to the state of the technology and standards around **2026**.

The purpose of reading this material is to give you a strong understanding of:

* how this type of platform is commonly architected;
* which standards and protocols are relevant;
* which architectural patterns are established;
* which responsibilities are normally separated;
* which interoperability models exist;
* which security models are relevant;
* which deployment patterns are commonly used;
* which constraints and trade-offs are known;
* which terminology and concepts are important;
* which architectural approaches have proven useful;
* which approaches should be treated with caution.

---

# 2. RESEARCH IS CONTEXT, NOT THE ARCHITECTURE

The content of `/research` is **reference material only**.

You MUST NOT treat the research material as the architecture of this project.

In particular:

> RESEARCH ≠ REQUIREMENT

> RESEARCH ≠ ARCHITECTURAL DECISION

> RESEARCH ≠ IMPLEMENTATION PLAN

> RESEARCH ≠ TECHNOLOGY MANDATE

The research exists to help you understand the problem space before making architectural decisions.

---

# 3. DO NOT COPY THE RESEARCH ARCHITECTURE

Do not blindly reproduce architectures, diagrams, components or technology choices found in the research.

A research document may describe:

* another organization's architecture;
* another scale;
* another security model;
* another deployment environment;
* another business requirement;
* another technology stack;
* another operational model;
* another set of constraints.

Therefore, every research-derived idea must be evaluated against the actual requirements of this platform.

The fact that a pattern appears in an authoritative source does NOT automatically mean that we should use it.

---

# 4. PURPOSE OF THE RESEARCH PHASE

The research phase has one primary purpose:

> **UNDERSTAND BEFORE DESIGNING.**

Before executing the architecture prompts, build a mental model of the current architectural landscape.

You should understand:

```text
STANDARDS
    ↓
PROTOCOLS
    ↓
ARCHITECTURAL PATTERNS
    ↓
RESPONSIBILITY BOUNDARIES
    ↓
SECURITY MODELS
    ↓
INTEROPERABILITY MODELS
    ↓
DEPLOYMENT PATTERNS
    ↓
KNOWN TRADE-OFFS
```

Then use that understanding while executing the actual architecture prompts.

---

# 5. DO NOT PRE-DESIGN THE PLATFORM

Do not use the research phase to prematurely design the final platform.

Do not decide before executing the prompts:

* the final component boundaries;
* the final technology stack;
* the final deployment topology;
* the final communication protocols;
* the final database architecture;
* the final agent architecture;
* the final model architecture;
* the final security architecture.

Those decisions must emerge progressively from:

```text
REQUIREMENTS
    ↓
RESEARCH CONTEXT
    ↓
ARCHITECTURE PROMPT
    ↓
ANALYSIS
    ↓
ALTERNATIVES
    ↓
TRADE-OFFS
    ↓
ARCHITECTURAL DECISION
```

---

# 6. RESEARCH SHOULD INFORM, NOT CONTROL

While executing each architecture prompt, use the research knowledge when it is relevant.

For example, research may reveal:

* an established standard;
* an interoperability protocol;
* a security pattern;
* a known architectural boundary;
* a known failure mode;
* a proven deployment model;
* a known scalability constraint;
* an established governance approach.

Use this knowledge to improve the analysis.

However, always ask:

> "Does this actually fit our requirements?"

If the answer is no, do not use it simply because it is an established pattern.

---

# 7. AUTHORITATIVE SOURCES

Give greater weight to authoritative sources such as:

* official standards;
* standards organizations;
* official specifications;
* official protocol documentation;
* official technical documentation;
* recognized industry specifications;
* primary technical sources;
* authoritative security publications.

Avoid treating:

* blog posts;
* marketing material;
* vendor claims;
* opinion pieces;
* unsourced diagrams;
* generic tutorials

as equivalent to authoritative standards.

They may still provide useful context, but their authority must be evaluated appropriately.

---

# 8. RESEARCH VS CURRENT VERIFICATION

The `/research` directory represents the research baseline available for this project.

However, it does not replace current verification when a decision depends on information that may have changed.

If a technology or standard is relevant to a decision and its current status matters, verify it separately when necessary.

Examples:

* current specification version;
* current implementation support;
* current API capabilities;
* current security status;
* current compatibility;
* current licensing;
* current maturity;
* current vendor support.

Distinguish clearly between:

### RESEARCH CONTEXT

What we learned from `/research`.

### CURRENT FACT

What has been independently verified as current.

### INFERENCE

What we conclude from those facts.

### ARCHITECTURAL DECISION

What we decide to use in this platform.

---

# 9. RESEARCH TRACEABILITY

When research materially influences an architectural decision, record that influence.

The decision should be understandable as:

```text
Research / Standard
        ↓
Relevant architectural insight
        ↓
Impact on our requirements
        ↓
Alternatives considered
        ↓
Decision
```

Do not simply write:

> "The standard recommends X, therefore we use X."

Instead explain:

> "The research identifies X as the established approach for Y. In our architecture this is relevant because Z. We evaluated X against A and B and selected it because..."

---

# 10. RESEARCH MUST NOT CREATE ARTIFICIAL COMPLEXITY

Do not introduce components merely because the research describes them.

Do not introduce:

* additional services;
* additional brokers;
* additional databases;
* additional protocols;
* additional abstraction layers;
* additional security components;
* additional orchestration layers

unless they solve an actual requirement or architectural problem.

The principle remains:

> **AS SIMPLE AS POSSIBLE, BUT NO SIMPLER THAN THE REQUIREMENTS ALLOW.**

---

# 11. RESEARCH MUST NOT OVERRIDE THE ARCHITECTURE PROCESS

After the research orientation phase is complete, proceed normally with the architecture prompts.

The execution remains:

```text
READ /research
      ↓
UNDERSTAND THE ARCHITECTURAL LANDSCAPE
      ↓
LEVEL A
      ↓
LEVEL B
      ↓
LEVEL C
      ↓
CROSS-DOCUMENT VALIDATION
      ↓
CONFLICT DETECTION
      ↓
ARCHITECTURAL RE-EVALUATION
      ↓
FALSIFICATION
      ↓
OPTIMIZATION
      ↓
FINAL ARCHITECTURE
```

The research phase does NOT introduce a new architectural level.

It is a **preparatory context phase**.

---

# 12. DO NOT LET RESEARCH BIAS THE PROMPTS

Once the research phase is complete, execute the architecture prompts according to the existing Master Entrypoint rules.

Do not modify the execution order because of the research.

Do not skip a prompt because the research appears to answer it.

Do not assume a decision has already been made.

Each prompt must still be independently analyzed.

The research provides context.

The prompt provides the architectural question.

The requirements provide the constraints.

Claude makes the decision.

---

# 13. RESEARCH CONFLICTS

If two authoritative research sources disagree:

Do not silently choose one.

Record the conflict and evaluate:

* scope;
* version;
* maturity;
* applicability;
* assumptions;
* security implications;
* operational implications;
* implementation support.

Then determine which information is relevant to this platform.

If the conflict cannot be resolved:

> `UNRESOLVED`

Do not fabricate consensus.

---

# 14. RESEARCH DOES NOT FREEZE TECHNOLOGY

The research directory may mention specific technologies.

Those technologies are candidates, not mandatory choices.

Never assume:

* a specific cloud;
* a specific database;
* a specific message broker;
* a specific container platform;
* a specific model provider;
* a specific GPU vendor;
* a specific orchestration system;
* a specific protocol implementation

simply because they appear in the research.

The architectural requirement must come first.

---

# 15. RESEARCH KNOWLEDGE MUST BE USED SELECTIVELY

Do not dump research content into the architecture documents.

Do not reproduce large portions of research.

Do not turn the final architecture into a literature review.

Use the research only where it helps answer an architectural question.

The final documentation must describe:

> **OUR ARCHITECTURE**

not:

> "what the research says about architectures."

---

# 16. FINAL PRINCIPLE

The research phase exists to prevent uninformed architectural decisions.

It does NOT exist to predetermine the architecture.

Therefore:

> **READ THE RESEARCH.**

> **UNDERSTAND THE STANDARDS.**

> **LEARN FROM ESTABLISHED ARCHITECTURAL PATTERNS.**

> **VERIFY CURRENT FACTS WHEN NECESSARY.**

> **THEN THINK FOR YOURSELF.**

The final architecture must be derived from the combination of:

```text
ACTUAL REQUIREMENTS
+
RESEARCH KNOWLEDGE
+
CURRENT VERIFIED FACTS
+
ARCHITECTURAL ANALYSIS
+
TRADE-OFFS
+
SECURITY
+
RELIABILITY
+
SCALABILITY
+
OPERABILITY
+
IMPLEMENTABILITY
```

The research is a **compass**, not a blueprint.

After reading `/research`, proceed normally with the existing Master Entrypoint and execute the architecture prompts sequentially:

```text
LEVEL A
→
LEVEL B
→
LEVEL C
```

Do not stop after the research phase.

Do not produce the final architecture during the research phase.

The research phase exists only to make the subsequent architectural reasoning **better informed, more rigorous and more aware of the 2026 technical landscape**.
